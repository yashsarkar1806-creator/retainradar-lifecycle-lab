RetainRadar Lifecycle Lab

Drop in an orders export and get RFM segments, a cohort retention heatmap, a simple CLV, and a lifecycle campaign plan per segment (onboarding, VIP, win-back) with margin guardrails and holdout sizing. Then pressure-test subject lines with rules and an optional on-device AI pass.

Everything runs in your browser. Your CSV never leaves this page. The demo uses synthetic orders for a fictional DTC tea brand, Kettle & Kin.

1 · Orders data

One row per order (or per line item, which gets grouped by order ID). Needs a customer ID, an order date (YYYY-MM-DD, DD/MM/YYYY or MM/DD/YYYY, detected automatically) and an order value. Refunds and zero-value rows are excluded.

      4 · Cohort retention

      Share of each first-order month's customers who ordered again in month +k. Blank cells are months that haven't happened yet. The last row is a size-weighted average.

      5 · Customer lifetime value

      Simple CLV = AOV × orders per year × gross margin × expected lifespan. Lifespan = 1 ÷ annual churn (capped at 0.5–5 years). Churn = share of customers older than the churn window who haven't ordered within it. Use it to set a CAC ceiling (3:1 LTV:CAC).

      Median days between repeat orders: – (75th percentile –). This sets reminder and win-back timing below.

      6 · Lifecycle campaign planner

      One program per segment, ranked by estimated net incremental gross profit for one campaign cycle: incremental orders × AOV × (margin − discount), minus the discount handed to people who'd have bought anyway, minus send costs. Base rates and lifts are editable planning assumptions in lib/engine.js. Validate them with the holdout.

      7 · Subject-line lab rules + transformers.js

      Rules score length for mobile, personalisation, numbers, spam triggers, caps and emoji, and whether the message angle fits the segment (for example, no discount-led lines to Champions).

      Optional AI pass runs open Hugging Face models in your browser with transformers.js: Xenova/mobilebert-uncased-mnli uses zero-shot classification to label each line's angle, and to rank a bank of 21 template ideas by how well they fit the chosen segment (blended 75% rules, 25% AI). The first run downloads about 25 MB, which the browser then caches. If the model can't load, the rule-based results still work and the template suggestions are used instead.
